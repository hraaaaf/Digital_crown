## Goal
Classify fluoride/remineralization families without conflating international clinical evidence, Morocco market presence, and Morocco regulatory validation.

## International clinical evidence

### Topical fluorides
ADA evidence-based guidance supports, for patients at caries risk, professional 2.26% fluoride varnish, 1.23% APF gel, prescription-strength home 0.5% fluoride gel/paste and 0.09% fluoride mouthrinse in age-appropriate patients; for children under 6, the ADA guideline specifically recommends 2.26% fluoride varnish among the evaluated professional/prescription agents. AAPD fluoride best practice separately covers varnish, APF gel/foam, SDF, toothpaste and mouthrinse with patient-specific risk assessment.

Classification: CLINICAL_FAMILY_SUPPORTED; product/age/risk/protocol specific; Morocco product validation separate.

### SDF
AAPD supports SDF as part of an ongoing individualized caries-management plan and its evidence-based pediatric guideline addresses 38% SDF for arresting cavitated caries lesions. ADA nonrestorative guidance also includes 38% SDF among lesion-specific options.

Classification: CLINICAL_FAMILY_SUPPORTED; PROFESSIONAL/PROTOCOL_SPECIFIC; Morocco exact product/regulatory status remains TO_VERIFY_MA.

### CPP-ACP / CPP-ACPF
The ADA nonrestorative caries guidance includes a recommendation against 10% CPP-ACP paste for certain noncavitated coronal lesions when other fluoride interventions, sealants, or resin infiltration are accessible. Therefore CPP-ACP must not be represented as a universal first-line remineralization substitute for fluoride.

GC product information confirms RECALDENT/CPP-ACP is derived from milk casein and is contraindicated in patients with milk-protein or hydroxybenzoate allergy. ASCIA independently advises patients with cow's-milk allergy to avoid dental products containing Recaldent/CPP-ACP.

Classification: EVIDENCE_CONTEXT_DEPENDENT + MILK_PROTEIN_ALLERGY_GUARD; no automatic clinical recommendation.

### Hydroxyapatite / nano-hydroxyapatite
A 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis reports expanding clinical/in-situ evidence for hydroxyapatite oral-care products. However, a 2025 Evidence-Based Dentistry commentary explicitly questions whether evidence is sufficient for recommendation. Major ADA/AAPD fluoride guidance continues to provide established fluoride recommendations. Therefore no fluoride-equivalence claim is accepted in this dataset.

Classification: EMERGING_EVIDENCE / NO_FLUORIDE_EQUIVALENCE_CLAIM.
